Construction Estimating Glossary

Common terms used in construction cost estimating — from project types to trade-specific language.

Hard Costs
Direct construction costs — materials and labor for all physical building work. Includes foundation, framing, MEP, roofing, and finishing. Typically 70–80% of total project cost.
Soft Costs
Indirect project costs not tied to physical construction. Includes architectural fees, engineering, permits, surveys, legal fees, and financing. Usually 10–20% of total.
Contingency
A reserve percentage built into estimates to cover unexpected costs, scope changes, and unknowns. Standard contingency is 10% for residential; 15–20% for complex or custom projects.
MEP
Mechanical, Electrical, and Plumbing — the three major trade systems in any building. Often estimated separately due to the significant cost and specialized subcontractors involved.
General Conditions
GC project overhead costs not tied to specific trades: site supervision, temporary facilities, dumpsters, portable restrooms, safety equipment, and project management time.
Overhead & Profit (O&P)
The GC's markup applied to total project costs. Overhead covers business operating costs (8–15%); profit is the contractor's margin (10–20%). Combined O&P typically runs 18–35%.
Bid Bond
A surety bond guaranteeing that a contractor will honor their bid if selected. Common in commercial work. Typically 1–3% of the bid amount, issued by a surety company.
Allowance
A placeholder dollar amount in a bid for items not yet fully specified — typically owner-selected finishes like flooring, fixtures, or appliances. Allowances can shift significantly.
Change Order
A formal amendment to the original contract scope and price. Change orders can increase or decrease the contract sum and timeline. All changes should be documented in writing.
Takeoff
The process of measuring and quantifying materials from construction plans — counting linear feet, square footage, cubic yards, and units needed for each trade.
Unit Price
A cost expressed per measurable unit (e.g., per square foot, per linear foot, per door). Unit pricing allows fast scaling across project sizes and is the basis of SSC Estimating's cost engine.
Scope of Work
A written description defining exactly what is — and isn't — included in a contract or bid. Clear scope prevents disputes, change orders, and budget overruns on both sides.
